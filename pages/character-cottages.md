          ## Historic Stone Cottages in Garsdale

          All three of our holiday cottages in Garsdale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, are stone cottages, and two of them have a documented history going back centuries. [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) was a cart house and stable, recorded in the 1845 survey, and Jack and Loraine converted it using reclaimed materials. [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/) was built in 1675 as the blacksmith's cottage and workshop and is furnished with antiques. [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) is a stone cottage on the dale's old list of house names, with exposed stone walls and beams and a modern kitchen and bathrooms.

          If you would rather stay in a character cottage than a new-build holiday let, ours are self-catering and let by the owners who look after them, between Sedbergh and Hawes with walks from the door.

          ## The Old Cart House: A Restored Cart House and Stable

          [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) was built to house carts and horses, and there is an Ordnance Survey benchmark cut into the stonework from the 1845 survey, when it was listed as J Davis' stable. Jack and Loraine renovated Rose Cottage next door first, then converted the cart house into a one-bedroom cottage for two, and a lot of what went into it was reclaimed. The door from the bedroom to the en-suite is an old stable door, the living room has beams under a vaulted ceiling, and one guest wrote that it has "lots of original features from the building's history".

          The furnishings at this holiday cottage include rag rugs on the floor, quilts on the bed, bedding in colours rather than hotel white, and big bath sheets instead of small towels, and guests have noticed the crockery and the espresso cups. A toy red squirrel in a knitted scarf sits on the stair railing. There is a written history of the building in the cottage to read, and one guest who met Jack heard the story of how he and Loraine came to have the place and how they renovated it.

          ## Farrier's Cottage: The 1675 Blacksmith's Cottage

          [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/) is a semi-detached stone cottage that began life in 1675 as the blacksmith's cottage and workshop. The walls are thick stone, the floors are solid wood and slate, and the windows look straight out across the dale.

          The house is furnished with antiques. The dining room has a grandfather clock, a writing bureau and a solid wooden table that seats six, the main bedroom has an iron and brass bedstead, and the twin room is up under the old roof beams with a rocking chair in the corner. The sitting room has a woodburning stove set in a big exposed-stone fireplace, with books and DVDs on the shelves either side of the chimney breast. Farrier's Cottage sleeps six, and Phil, who owns it, takes enquiries through the form on the cottage page.

          ## Roger Pot: A Stone Cottage with Beams and a Modern Kitchen

          [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) appears on the old list of house names for the middle of the dale, alongside Rose Cottage and Pinfold Farm, and it sits up a farm track above the river. The sitting room has exposed stone walls, wooden roof beams and a woodburning stove, and the master bedroom has beams too. Paul and Karen have fitted it out with an induction hob, a dishwasher, underfloor heating downstairs and an en-suite walk-in shower alongside the original stone walls and beams. It sleeps six across three bedrooms, and Paul bakes bread and cake for every guest.

          ## The History Around the Cottages

          Garsdale is old farming country, with Norse place names, a medieval church site, three Methodist chapels and the Settle-Carlisle railway running across the top of the dale. Our [history of Garsdale](/garsdale-history/) page covers it in more detail, from the Vikings to the Great Flood of 1889, and [Garsdale house names and local places](/garsdale-house-names-and-local-places/) goes through the old names of the farms and cottages from one end of the dale to the other. [Farming in Garsdale](/farming-in-garsdale/) explains the sheep and the farming year, and [Churches and Chapels](/churches-and-chapels/) covers St John the Baptist and the chapels.

          For a walk with some history in it, the [Sedgwick Geological Trail](/the-sedgwick-geological-trail/) at Danny Bridge follows the Dent Fault along the River Clough and is named after Adam Sedgwick, the geologist, who was born in nearby Dent. [Grisedale](/grisedale/), the next valley along, was filmed in 1975 as The Dale That Died and is a short drive or a longer walk away.

          ## Good to Know Before You Book

          Old stone cottages come with low beams and old staircases. At The Old Cart House the beams are low in places, the rear door from the downstairs room is low enough that you will need to mind your head, and the bedroom and en-suite shower room are both upstairs, with no bathroom on the ground floor. The garden at Farrier's Cottage is not enclosed and a public right of way crosses the front of the house. Roger Pot is reached by a 400 metre farm track with gates to open. Each cottage page lists its full details.
