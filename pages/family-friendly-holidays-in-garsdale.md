          ## Family Holidays in Garsdale

          Garsdale is a quiet valley in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, between Sedbergh and Hawes, and it works well for a family holiday. There is a river to paddle in below Roger Pot, red squirrels in the woodland at Dandra Garth, footpaths that start at the door, and a station on the Settle-Carlisle line a few minutes up the dale. In spring the curlews come inland to nest and you can hear them calling from the fields.

          Our self-catering holiday cottages sleep from two to six people, so a larger family or two families sharing have somewhere to stay without splitting up. [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) and [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/) both sleep six across three bedrooms. [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) sleeps two and is the one for a couple.

          The three cottages are owned separately, so you deal with the person who owns and runs the house rather than a call centre. Paul, who owns Roger Pot, lives in the barn next door, which is handy if a gate is stuck or the stove needs explaining. That matters more than usual with children, because the questions tend to be practical ones about gates, stairs, cot space and how far the nearest shop is.

          ## Family Friendly Holiday Cottages in Garsdale

          [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) is a detached stone holiday cottage a quarter of a mile up a farm track from the road, with three bedrooms and space for six. There is a super-king bedroom with an en-suite shower room, a double, and a twin that can be made up as a super-king if you ask the owner in advance. That last one is useful with children, since it can be two singles for the kids or another double for a second couple. The sitting room has a woodburning stove, and there is a separate dining room so the table is not also the kitchen worktop. A highchair is available, the utility room has a washer/dryer, and the enclosed side garden has a lawn and outdoor furniture.

          [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/) is the other one that suits a larger group. It began life in 1675 as the blacksmith's cottage and workshop, and it sleeps six across a king-size double, a second double and a twin up under the roof beams. There is a shower room on the ground floor as well as the family bathroom upstairs, which saves a queue in the morning. A travel cot and a highchair are available, there is somewhere dry to put the bikes, and off-road parking for two cars.

          [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) sleeps two, so it is the one for a couple rather than a family. If your group is bigger than six, the three cottages can be booked together for up to fourteen guests, though they are owned separately and availability has to be coordinated between the owners.

          Both of the six-berth cottages are dog friendly, and Roger Pot takes up to two well-behaved dogs. See our [dog friendly cottages page](/dog-friendly-cottages-in-garsdale-or-yorkshire-dales-stays/) if the dog is coming too.

          ## Family Days Out Near Garsdale

          [The Forbidden Corner](/places/the-forbidden-corner-middleham/) at Middleham, near Leyburn, is a garden of tunnels, chambers, follies and surprises set in four acres. It is aimed at primary-age children rather than toddlers or teenagers, and it sits across the dale from Hawes, so a visit can be combined with [Aysgarth Falls](/places/aysgarth-falls/) or [Bolton Castle](/places/bolton-castle/) on the same day.

          [White Scar Cave](/places/white-scar-cave/) near Ingleton is the longest show cave in Britain, with guided tours of about eighty minutes through a mile of passages. The cave stays at 8°C all year, so take a jumper even in July, and the electric lighting and guide make it a reasonable outing with children who are not keen on the dark. It pairs well with the [Ingleton Waterfalls Trail](/places/ingleton-waterfalls-trail/), which is a four and a half mile circular route taking between two and a half and four hours. That one is for older children with decent boots.

          [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/) in Hawes runs cheesemaking demonstrations and lets you taste the cheeses, and the [Dales Countryside Museum](/places/dales-countryside-museum-hawes/) in the same town has family events through the year. [Bolton Castle](/places/bolton-castle/) is worth the drive for the Mary Queen of Scots connection and the views, and [Brough Castle](/places/brough-castle/) and [Pendragon Castle](/places/pendragon-castle/) are free ruins in Mallerstang that children can run around. For older children who want something faster, [Go Ape](/places/go-ape-grizedale-forest/) at Grizedale Forest and the zip line at [Honister Slate Mine](/places/honister-slate-mine/) are both in the Lake District.

          ## Rainy Days with Children

          Rain is part of the deal in the Dales, so it is worth having somewhere indoors in mind before you set off. [Farfield Mill](/places/farfield-mill-sedbergh/) is on the road between Garsdale and Sedbergh, with exhibitions, craft work and a cafe, and it fills a wet morning without a long drive. Hawes has the [Dales Countryside Museum](/places/dales-countryside-museum-hawes/) and the [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/), both of which have something for children to watch. Kendal has the [Brewery Arts Centre](/places/brewery-arts-centre-kendal/) with its cinema, music and comedy, and children's daytime events in the holidays.

          [White Scar Cave](/places/white-scar-cave/) is underground and lit, so the weather does not matter once you are inside. [Hardraw Force](/places/hardraw-force/) is a short walk on laid-out paths behind the [Green Dragon](/places/green-dragon-inn-hardraw/), with a small entrance fee, and the paths there are level and built up.

          ## Walks and Outdoor Days

          There are [walks from the door](/walks/) that work with children. The [Sedgwick Geological Trail](/the-sedgwick-geological-trail/) along the River Clough at Danny Bridge is short and follows the river, with rocks and a fault line to look at. [Cotter Force](/places/cotter-force/) has a parking area nearby and a flat pathway beside the river to the waterfall. [Hardraw Force](/places/hardraw-force/) is similarly easy underfoot, with seating along the way. [Cautley Spout](/places/cautley-spout/) is a longer walk with some inclines, so it is one for older legs and a dry day.

          The two local caves, [Tom Croft Cave](/places/tom-croft-cave/) and [Dovecote Cave](/places/dovecote-cave/), can be explored with a torch. [Semerwater](/places/semerwater/) is the closest lake, in Raydale, with footpaths around the shore and space for a picnic.

          The river below Roger Pot is shallow enough for paddling and for fishing, and children need supervising there at all times. The red squirrels are easiest to see on the path beside Dandra Garth and on the riverbank opposite, and they occasionally come to the garden feeders at the cottages. On a clear night the [dark skies](/dark-skies-in-garsdale/) are worth staying up for, since there is no street lighting in the dale and the Milky Way is visible to the naked eye.

          [Garsdale Station](/places/garsdale-station/) on the Settle-Carlisle line is a few minutes up the dale. The run north over the viaducts to Kirkby Stephen and Carlisle, or south towards Settle, is one of the great railway journeys in England and needs no planning beyond checking the timetable.

          ## Practical Information for Families

          A few things are worth knowing before you arrive with children.

          **The farm track at Roger Pot** is 400 metres long, single width, with two or three gates to open for the owner's horses, and it is steep and uneven in places because of the drainage channels. A normal car manages it, but very low profile cars are better left on the road. Because the track is single width, the owner runs a simple texting system so that guests and the owner know when someone is coming up or down.

          **The garden at Farrier's Cottage** is not enclosed, and there is a public right of way across the front of the house, so small children and dogs need watching. Roger Pot's side garden is enclosed.

          **The water at Roger Pot** comes from a nearby well. It is tested and treated every year, but it cannot be guaranteed, so bottled water is supplied for drinking and cooking, with free top-ups during your stay.

          **The river** at Roger Pot is within the owner's land and children must be supervised at all times.

          **Dogs** need to be on a lead anywhere near stock, since this is sheep farming country. Roger Pot takes up to two well-behaved dogs, Farrier's Cottage takes one, and dogs cannot be left alone in either cottage.

          **The nearest shop** is in Sedbergh, about six miles west, so it is worth arriving with the basics. Sedbergh has a Spar and [Powells](/places/powells-sedbergh/) for fruit and vegetables, and a market on Wednesday mornings. Hawes is 9 miles east with a market on Tuesdays.

          ## Eating Out with Children

          The pubs and cafes within easy reach are listed in full on our [eating and drinking page](/eating-and-drinking-in-garsdale/). The closest to the cottages are the [Green Dragon](/places/green-dragon-inn-hardraw/) at Hardraw, which serves food and has a music night on Wednesdays, the [White Hart](/places/white-hart-inn-hawes/) and the [Board Inn](/places/board-inn-hawes/) in Hawes, and the [Red Lion](/places/red-lion-sedbergh/) in Sedbergh. The [Cross Keys](/places/cross-keys-temperance-inn-cautley/) at Cautley is a temperance inn, so there is no alcohol, and [Stone Close tea room](/places/stone-close-tea-room-dent/) in Dent is a good stop after a day out.

          Food service times move about with the season round here, so it is worth ringing ahead if you are driving over specially with hungry children in the car.
