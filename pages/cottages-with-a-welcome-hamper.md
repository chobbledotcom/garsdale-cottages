          ## A Welcome Hamper at Our Garsdale Holiday Cottages

          At two of our holiday cottages in Garsdale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park near Sedbergh, there is a welcome hamper waiting when you arrive. At [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/), Paul bakes bread and cake for every guest and leaves the fridge stocked with the basics, and at [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/), Jack and Loraine leave a basket of local treats for every guest. More than a third of our guest reviews mention it, which is more than mention the views.

          ## Why a Welcome Hamper Matters Up Here

          A welcome hamper makes a practical difference at a holiday cottage in Garsdale, because the nearest shops are in Sedbergh, six or seven miles away, and after a long drive up the motorway most people would rather not turn round and go back out for milk and bread. With the hamper there is something to eat with the first cup of tea, and the shopping can wait until the morning.

          ## Paul's Homemade Bread and Parkin at Roger Pot

          Paul, who owns [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) with Karen, lives in the barn next door and bakes for every guest. What is in the hamper changes, but guests have found a loaf of his homemade bread (one guest had a sourdough), a parkin or a ginger cake, and some have had scones. Guests have also found milk, butter, eggs and cheese in the fridge and fresh flowers on the dining table, and one found local food in the basket as well.

          Parkin is a sticky ginger and oatmeal cake made with treacle, and it has been baked in Yorkshire and Lancashire for a long time, especially around Bonfire Night. Several guests have mentioned Paul's parkin by name in their reviews.

          ## Local Ale and Treats at The Old Cart House

          At [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/), the one-bedroom cottage that sleeps two, Jack and Loraine leave a welcome basket of local produce for every guest. Guests have found local ale in it, biscuits, cereal, mint cake and homemade jam, with tea, coffee and sugar for the morning. Kendal mint cake is made seventeen miles away in Kendal, and it went up Everest with the 1953 expedition.

          It is a good idea to bring milk and anything else you want for breakfast, since the basket is mostly treats and the nearest shop is in Sedbergh. Jack and Loraine take single-night bookings as well as longer stays, so the cottage works if you are just breaking a journey.

          ## Stocking Up for the Rest of Your Stay

          Once the hamper is finished, [Sedbergh](/locations/sedbergh/) is the nearest place to shop, with a Spar, [Powells](/places/powells-sedbergh/) for fruit and vegetables, [The Meat Hook](/places/the-meat-hook-sedbergh/), a family-run butcher that was Cumbrian Butcher of the Year in 2023, and a produce market on Wednesday mornings. [Hawes](/locations/hawes/) has a market on Tuesdays and the [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/), where you can taste the cheese before you buy it. [Kendal](/locations/kendal/) has the big supermarkets, about half an hour away.

          If you would rather not shop at all, Tesco, Morrisons, Asda and Sainsbury's all deliver to the cottages, and our [home deliveries](/places/home-deliveries/) page explains how. Our [eating and drinking](/eating-and-drinking-in-garsdale/) page covers the pubs and tea rooms, and has a section on Cumbrian food traditions, from rum butter to Cumberland sausage.
