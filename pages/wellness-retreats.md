          ## A Place to Unwind in the Yorkshire Dales
          Garsdale is one of the quietest corners of the Yorkshire Dales National Park. There is not much through traffic to speak of and no street lighting, and most nights there is nothing louder than an owl or the River Clough. It is a haven for [red squirrels](/red-squirrels-in-garsdale/), [otters and upland birds](/garsdale-wildlife/), and all three of our holiday cottages work well for guests looking for a wellness break, a digital detox or a gentle walking holiday.

          ## [Cautley Spout](/places/cautley-spout/) and the Cross Keys
          A short drive from Garsdale brings you to the **Cross Keys** at Cautley, a National Trust-owned temperance inn that serves no alcohol, only home-cooked food and tea. It sits below **Cautley Spout**, a 650-foot cascade down the eastern flank of the Howgill Fells and one of the longest waterfalls in England. The walk to the foot of the falls is gentle, and a steeper path carries on up onto the Howgills if you want more.
          For a slow wellness day, you can walk in beside the beck, feel the cold air coming off the water, and have a pot of tea and an unhurried lunch at the inn, which has no bar, before walking back out again. See our [eating and drinking](/eating-and-drinking-in-garsdale/) page for details.

          ## Walks for Mind and Body
          For a slow morning walk, the riverside path along the River Clough is flat, quiet and full of birdsong. For something a little more demanding, head up into [Grisedale](/grisedale/), known as "the dale that died", a quiet side valley where you will rarely meet another walker. The Howgill Fells above Sedbergh have open, smooth-topped summits with long views in every direction, and [The Sedgwick Geological Trail](/the-sedgwick-geological-trail/) along the River Clough at Danny Bridge makes a gentle half-day out. Our [walks](/walks/) page has many more routes for every level.
          A short drive over the Coal Road brings you into Dentdale, on the far side of Aye Gill Pike and about 26 minutes by car, for riverside walks from the village of Dent and a pot of tea in one of its tea rooms.

          ## Dark Skies Over Baugh Fell
          Garsdale sits within the Yorkshire Dales, which together with the North York Moors forms the largest area of protected dark sky in England. With no street lighting anywhere in the dale, the night sky is remarkable. On a clear night the Milky Way is visible to the naked eye, and **Baugh Fell** to the north makes a particularly dramatic backdrop for stargazing. Meteor showers, satellites and the occasional aurora can all be seen from the gardens of our three holiday cottages without having to drive anywhere. Our [dark skies in Garsdale](/dark-skies-in-garsdale/) page has more on what you can see and the best times of year.
          Our [Garsdale Wildlife](/garsdale-wildlife/) page has more on the dale's skies, birds and mammals.

          ## Arrive the Slow Way
          Perhaps the gentlest way to start a retreat is to leave the car behind. [Garsdale Station](/places/garsdale-station/) sits on the Settle-Carlisle line, one of the most scenic railway journeys in Britain, a few minutes' drive up the dale from our cottages. There is no bus or taxi from the station into Garsdale, so talk to the owners about getting from the platform to the cottage before you book. The bronze statue of [Ruswarp](/ruswarp-and-the-settle-carlisle/), the Border Collie who helped save the Settle-Carlisle line, sits on the southbound platform.
