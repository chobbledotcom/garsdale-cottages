          ## A Secluded Holiday Cottage in Garsdale

          [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) is a detached stone holiday cottage in Garsdale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, set a quarter of a mile up a farm track from the road, on the hillside above the River Clough. It sits within the owners' four acres, with fields and woodland round it and views across the dale from almost every window. Paul and Karen, who own it, live in the barn next door. It is a dog friendly, self-catering holiday cottage that sleeps six across three bedrooms, with a woodburning stove, an enclosed garden and room for up to two dogs at no extra charge.

          Of the seventy-odd reviews Roger Pot has had, about two in five mention the views, and around one in five mention how peaceful and quiet it is up there. If you want a secluded cottage in the Yorkshire Dales, this is the one of our three that fits, and our [peace and tranquillity](/peace-and-tranquillity-in-garsdale/) page covers the quiet of the dale more generally.

          > "We loved the seclusion and this was not an issue for us, but may not suit everyone."
          >
          > *Carol, Roger Pot*

          ## Views from Every Window at Roger Pot

          Roger Pot is up on the side of the dale rather than down by the road, so the views go a long way. The dining room off the kitchen looks out over the dale, and the master bedroom upstairs has low windows that one guest said gave the best view there is from the cottage. There is a table with six chairs out on the grass for eating outside when the weather allows, and an enclosed side garden with a lawn.

          The photographs on the cottage page show the rooms, but several guests have said they do not do the place justice, particularly the master bedroom and the outlook from it.

          ## Four Acres, the River Clough and the Horses at Roger Pot

          The cottage stands in Paul and Karen's four acres, and guests have the run of a stretch of the River Clough just below the house, which is shallow enough for paddling in normal conditions and can be fished. Children need supervising by the water. There is a circular walk from the door through the woods and back along the river, which one guest with a dog made a morning habit of, and the bridleway by Dandra Garth goes on up through the woodland towards Dent.

          Paul's horses are in the fields round the house, and the gates on the track are there because of them. One family's daughter loved watching him bring the horses in, and another guest said the kids, and the adults, would love the sheep and the two horses.

          ## Owners on Site at Roger Pot, near Sedbergh and Hawes

          Paul and Karen live in the barn next door, and guests have written that they were helpful when needed and otherwise left them to it. Roger Pot has superfast broadband and WiFi, which matters here because mobile signal in the dale is patchy. [Sedbergh](/locations/sedbergh/), the nearest town, is 6.3 miles away, with pubs, shops and a Wednesday market, and [Hawes](/locations/hawes/) is 9 miles the other way. The [Lake District](/places/lake-district/) is close enough for a day trip, and [Garsdale Station](/places/garsdale-station/) on the Settle-Carlisle line is a few minutes up the dale.

          ## Wildlife and Dark Skies in Garsdale

          Red squirrels have been seen on the riverbanks below Roger Pot, and the woodland round Garsdale is one of England's last strongholds for the [red squirrel](/red-squirrels-in-garsdale/), with the paths by Dandra Garth another good place to look. Roe deer and brown hares are about in the fields, and there are owls at night and curlews in the spring. Our [wildlife in Garsdale](/garsdale-wildlife/) page has more.

          At night there is no street lighting anywhere in the dale and no lit road below the house, so on a clear night you can see the Milky Way from the garden. Our [dark skies](/dark-skies-in-garsdale/) page explains what to look for.

          ## The Track Up to Roger Pot

          The one thing to know about before you book Roger Pot is the farm track. It is 400 metres long, single width, and steep and uneven in places because of the drainage channels, with two or three gates to open and close for the horses. Paul and Karen run a simple texting system with guests so that nobody meets a car coming the other way. A normal car manages it, and guests with low sports cars and estate cars have got up without trouble, but very low profile cars are better left on the road at the bottom. When it snowed over one New Year, guests left their cars at the bottom and walked up on Paul's advice, and driving it at night in heavy rain is harder than in daylight, so arrive before dark if you can.

          One guest mentioned a steep drop to the river on one side of the track, and an older couple who came in a wet October week found it hard going, so if the track worries you, talk to Paul before you book.

          > "I'm not a confident driver and I really didn't find it a problem doing it on my own and the gates too."
          >
          > *Shelley, Roger Pot*

          ## What to Bring to a Secluded Holiday Cottage

          You will need a car, since public transport up the dale is very limited and there are normally no taxis. The nearest shops are in Sedbergh, 6.7 miles away, so bring groceries for the first day, though Paul leaves homemade bread, cake and the basics in the fridge for every guest. Supermarket deliveries come to the bottom of the track, where the vans are met at the gate, and our [home deliveries](/places/home-deliveries/) page explains how. The water comes from a well that is tested and treated every year, and bottled water is supplied for drinking and cooking, with free top-ups.
