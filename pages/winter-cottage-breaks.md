          ## Winter Holiday Cottages in Garsdale

          Garsdale is a quiet valley in the Yorkshire Dales National Park between Sedbergh and Hawes, and our three self-catering holiday cottages here are open through the winter. Two of them, [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) and [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/), have a woodburning stove and sleep six, and [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) sleeps two with electric central heating and an electric fire. All three are dog friendly, and there are walks from the door of each of them.

          Winter is when the dale is at its quietest. The footpaths are quiet, the fells above the valley often get snow at some point between December and March, and the nights are long and dark, with no street lighting anywhere in the dale. It rains a fair bit as well, and some days the cloud sits on the tops and does not lift, and on those days the woodburning stove gets lit early. Guests who have stayed in the colder months often mention how warm the houses were, along with the welcome hamper and the views.

          ## Keeping Warm in a Winter Cottage

          [Roger Pot](/properties/roger-pot/) has underfloor heating on the ground floor and a woodburning stove in a sitting room with exposed stone walls and roof beams, and the house is well insulated. There is a basket of logs for the stove when you arrive, and Paul and Karen, who own it, live in the barn next door. If you are not sure how the heating works, ask Paul, since one or two guests have found it takes some explaining, and the backup electric radiators can be switched on if you ask. The family bathroom has a bath with a shower over it.

          [Farrier's Cottage](/properties/farriers-cottage/) has thick stone walls from 1675, gas central heating and a woodburning stove in a big exposed-stone fireplace, with a pair of leather sofas in front of it. Fuel for the stove is included. The utility room has a flagged floor and opens off the garden, so wet boots and a muddy dog stop there.

          [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) is a one-bedroom stone cottage with electric central heating and an electric fire, and it has roadside parking, so there is no track to deal with in bad weather. A guest who stayed in very wintery weather said it was lovely and warm despite it.

          ## Winter Walks from the Cottages

          You do not need to go far for a winter walk. The bridleway beside Dandra Garth climbs through the woods from the middle of the dale and on towards Dent, and the woodland there is one of the better places to see [red squirrels](/red-squirrels-in-garsdale/). The paths along the River Clough are low level, and the [Sedgwick Geological Trail](/the-sedgwick-geological-trail/) at Danny Bridge, a short drive down the A684, is short enough for an afternoon when it gets dark early. [Cotter Force](/places/cotter-force/), towards Hawes, has a flat path beside the river to the waterfall, and [Hardraw Force](/places/hardraw-force/) is at its best after a few days of rain.

          The higher ground is another matter in winter. The [Howgill Fells](/places/howgill-fells/), Baugh Fell and the Pennine Bridleway above Garsdale Head are good walking in snow for people with the right gear and experience, though the paths across Rise Hill can be boggy after rain. The weather on the tops changes quickly and the daylight is short, so check the mountain forecast before you go, and see the weather section on our [walks page](/walks/).

          ## Winter Wildlife and Dark Skies

          Redwings and fieldfares arrive in the dale for the winter, and the bird feeders at the cottages are busy with siskins, redpolls and long-tailed tits. The [red squirrels](/red-squirrels-in-garsdale/) are around all year, and the [Snaizeholme red squirrel trail](/places/snaizeholme-red-squirrel-trail/) near Hawes is worth a trip on a dry day, though there are only two parking spaces, booked in advance through the National Park Centre in Hawes, so the Little White Bus from Hawes is the easier way in. Our [bird watching](/bird-watching-properties/) page has more on what turns up in each season.

          The long nights are the other reason to come in winter. With no street lighting in Garsdale and very little light from the towns, the sky on a clear night is very dark, and in winter you do not have to stay up late to see it. The Geminid meteor shower peaks in mid-December. Our [dark skies in Garsdale](/dark-skies-in-garsdale/) page explains what you can see and where to stand.

          ## Rainy Days Out near Sedbergh and Hawes

          On days when the weather keeps you off the hills, [White Scar Cave](/places/white-scar-cave/) near Ingleton stays at 8°C all year and opens at weekends through the winter, so check before you drive over. Hawes has the [Dales Countryside Museum](/places/dales-countryside-museum-hawes/) and the [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/), [Farfield Mill](/places/farfield-mill-sedbergh/) is on the road between Garsdale and Sedbergh with a cafe and exhibitions, and Sedbergh's bookshops are good for a wet hour or two. The [Brewery Arts Centre](/places/brewery-arts-centre-kendal/) in Kendal has a cinema. The pubs within reach are listed on our [eating and drinking](/eating-and-drinking-in-garsdale/) page.

          The [Settle-Carlisle railway](/places/garsdale-station/) from Garsdale Station is worth doing in winter too, since the views from the train over Ribblehead and the Eden valley do not depend on the weather being kind for walking.
