          ## A Cottage for a Couple with a Baby in Garsdale

          [The Old Cart House](/properties/the-old-cart-house/) is a one-bedroom stone holiday cottage in Garsdale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park near Sedbergh, and it takes a couple with a baby or a small child up to the age of three. If you have a baby and want a small, quiet holiday cottage in the Yorkshire Dales for the two of you rather than a big family house, this is the one of our three to look at. Jack and Loraine, who own it, can provide a travel cot or a small child's bed and a highchair if you ask when you book.

          Downstairs is one open-plan room with the kitchen, a dining table and an electric fire, and the king-size bedroom is upstairs, where one guest with a nine-month-old found there was enough space for the travel cot. It is a self-catering cottage with a washing machine, fridge freezer, microwave and oven, and Jack and Loraine take one-night stays as well as longer ones, so a short first trip away with a baby is easy to book online.

          > "The host also provided a high chair which was incredibly helpful."
          >
          > *Christie, The Old Cart House*

          ## Stairs, Steps and the Garden

          Because the cottage is an old building with a staircase, there are a few things to watch with a baby or toddler. The timber staircase goes up to the bedroom, and children up to three are welcome as long as they are supervised on the stairs. The en-suite shower room is upstairs off the bedroom, and there is no bathroom on the ground floor, and no bath, only a wide walk-in shower. A second door from the bedroom opens onto stone steps down to the patio, so keep that door shut. The garden at the back is not fully enclosed, and the front of the cottage is on the A684, which most guests find quiet but one found busy in high summer, so hold toddlers' hands at the front.

          A stair gate and a steriliser are not on the list of what is provided, so ask Jack and Loraine when you book, or bring your own. Their numbers are on the [cottage page](/properties/the-old-cart-house/).

          ## Nappies, Medicine and the Nearest Hospital

          The nearest shops are in [Sedbergh](/locations/sedbergh/), about 6.4 miles away, where there is a [Spar](/places/spar-sedbergh/) and a pharmacy on Main Street, now [Allied Pharmacy](/places/jhoots-pharmacy-sedbergh/). [Central Dales Pharmacy](/places/central-dales-pharmacy-hawes/) is in Hawes, nine miles the other way. Tesco, Morrisons, Asda and Sainsbury's all deliver to the cottages, so nappies and baby food can be ordered ahead, and our [home deliveries](/places/home-deliveries/) page explains how.

          [Westmorland General Hospital](/places/westmorland-general-hospital-kendal/) in Kendal has an Urgent Treatment Centre for non-life-threatening problems, about twenty-five minutes' drive away, and the [Royal Lancaster Infirmary](/places/royal-lancaster-infirmary-lancaster/) has the nearest full Accident and Emergency department. Mobile signal in the dale is patchy, but the cottage has WiFi, and our [getting here](/getting-here/) page has more on coverage.

          ## Days Out with a Baby

          A fair amount round here works with a pushchair. [Hardraw Force](/places/hardraw-force/) is a short walk on a level gravelled path behind the Green Dragon, and [Cotter Force](/places/cotter-force/), on the way to Hawes, has a flat path beside the river to the waterfall. Hawes has the [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/) and the [Dales Countryside Museum](/places/dales-countryside-museum-hawes/), and [Farfield Mill](/places/farfield-mill-sedbergh/), on the road between Garsdale and Sedbergh, has a cafe and exhibitions under one roof. Sedbergh has cafes and bookshops along its main street.

          The [Settle-Carlisle railway](/places/garsdale-station/) from Garsdale Station is a good day out with a baby, since you can sit with them on your knee and watch the viaducts and fells go past, though it is worth checking step-free access to the platforms on the [National Rail station page](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/stations/garsdale/) first. For the fell paths and anything rougher, including the woods at Dandra Garth, a baby carrier is better than a pushchair. [The Forbidden Corner](/places/the-forbidden-corner-middleham/) near Leyburn has very limited pushchair access, so take a carrier if you go.
