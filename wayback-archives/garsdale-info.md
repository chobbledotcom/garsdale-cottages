# Garsdale & Sedbergh: A Visitor's Guide

Notes compiled from archived pages of garsdale.info (via the Wayback Machine).

## The Dale

Garsdale is not a village but a valley, approximately seven miles long, through which runs the A684 road from Sedbergh to Hawes. A mile in the centre is road-signed "Garsdale" and includes "The Street." The other centre of population is at Garsdale Head, still locally known as "Hawes Junction" after the junction of the Wensleydale branch from the Settle-Carlisle railway.

To the north is Baugh Fell with its tarns. To the south is Rise Hill, then Dentdale. The River Clough runs through the dale and has its source in Grisedale, known as "The Dale that Died" -- a valley that lost its population. To locals, the whole six or seven miles from Garsdale Foot at the western end to the Moorcock and Mount Zion Chapel is just "Garsdale."

## Getting There

- **By rail**: Garsdale Station is on the famous Settle-Carlisle railway line (the station was originally called Hawes Junction). The Dandrymire Viaduct is visible from nearby Mount Zion Chapel.
- **By road**: The A684 runs through the valley connecting Sedbergh (to the west) to Hawes (to the east).
- **Taxis**: "Country Private Hire" operates from Moorcock House. Tel: 01969-667-096.

## Places to Visit

- **Farfield Mill** -- Arts and heritage centre near Sedbergh (farfieldmill.org).
- **Hardraw Force** -- England's highest single-drop waterfall, accessible through the Green Dragon pub at Hardraw.
- **Tom Croft Cave and Dovecote Cave** -- Local caves to explore (see Garsdale maps for locations).
- **Cotterdale Force** -- Waterfall as sketched by Turner.
- **Grisedale: "The Dale that Died"** -- The depopulated valley to the north, source of the River Clough. A traditional annual walk is organised from Mount Zion Chapel.

## Churches and Chapels

Garsdale has four places of worship, all open to visitors:

- **St John the Baptist's Church** (Anglican) -- Always open. The present church was built in 1861 on the site of an ancient chapel. Features a fine stained-glass east window by C.E. Kempe. In 1799, William Wordsworth wrote to Coleridge describing a walk with his sister Dorothy through Garsdale, mentioning "we rested in a tempting inn, close by a lowly house of prayer in a charming little valley." An annual united lambing service is held here (come and see the lambs in church).
- **Low Smithy Chapel** (Methodist) -- Open 8am to 5pm daily.
- **Garsdale Street Chapel** (Methodist) -- Open 8am to 5pm daily.
- **Mount Zion Chapel, Hawes Junction** (Methodist) -- Open 8am to 5pm daily. Built in 1876, the same year the Midland Railway opened the Settle-Carlisle line. Reuben Alton laid the foundation stone on the very day of the first passenger train. It was built by railway contractors Groves and Woodiwiss and is painted in Midland Railway colours (maroon and cream) -- believed to be the only place of worship in the country built by railway contractors. From its windows there are views of Garsdale Station and the Dandrymire Viaduct. The extension won an award from the Yorkshire Dales National Park. Features in the "Faiths Trail" published by the Churches Trust for Cumbria. Can be hired as a base for walkers (10 pounds per session or 25 pounds per day), with toilet, disabled access, kettles and water boiler.

A "Railways & Religion in the Western Dales" leaflet is available in the chapels.

## Wildlife: Red Squirrels

Garsdale is one of the last English strongholds for red squirrels, an endangered species. The area is part of the "Greenfields Stronghold" conservation effort, and the Garsdale Red Squirrel Group was launched in November 2014.

Red squirrels are regularly seen at numerous locations including Reachey (Grisedale), Mudbecks, Railway Cottages, Banks, East Littletown, Paradise, Dandra Garth, Coat Weggs, The Hive, The Firs, Rackenthwaite, Fold Gill, Whitbeck, and Nether House. They regularly cross the main road at Banks, The Street, The Firs and Whitbeck -- drivers should take care where warning notices are posted.

How to distinguish red from grey squirrels: reds are small and dainty with ear tufts (except in summer); greys are stocky with no ear tufts and a white "halo" around the tail.

Sightings can be reported to 015396 25453 or garsdaleredsquirrels.org.uk.

## Eating and Drinking

There is no public house in Garsdale itself. Garsdale Hall was formerly a coaching inn called the "George & Dragon" (recorded in the 1851 census), and Junction Cottage at Garsdale Head was formerly the Junction Inn.

Nearby options:
- **The Moorcock Inn** -- At the head of the dale, just into Richmondshire, at the junction of the A684 and B6259. Food and accommodation. Tel: 01969-667-488.
- **The Green Dragon at Hardraw** -- Tel: 01969-667-392.
- **The Old Joinery** -- Near the centre of Garsdale, offers cream teas. Tel: 015396-20309.
- **Sedbergh and Hawes** offer a wider range of pubs, restaurants and cafes.

### Local Food
- Wensleydale cheese used to be made at Farfield using milk from Garsdale, Dent and Cautley. The Wensleydale Creamery makes a variety called "Garsdale" with garlic and chives.
- Wild garlic grows in Garsdale and is locally known as "Ramps."

## Events and Festivals

Garsdale has a lively calendar of community events, many centred on the Village Hall:

- **Garsdale Sports** -- Annual event in May or June, from 1pm at the Village Hall field, with children's races and free tea for children.
- **Evening Walks and Barbecues** -- Summer months from the Village Hall.
- **Harvest Festivals** -- At each chapel in September/October, often featuring the Orton Male Voice Choir, supper and sale of produce.
- **Beetle Drive** -- October, at the Village Hall.
- **Garsdale Bonfire and Fireworks** -- Early November, lit at 7:15pm.
- **Domino Drive** -- Winter months at the Village Hall.
- **Carol Singing round the Houses** -- December, going from house to house over several evenings collecting for charity.
- **Carol Services** -- At each church and chapel in December.
- **Carols by Candlelight** -- At Mount Zion Chapel, with supper afterwards.
- **Lambing Service** -- April, at St John's Church (come and see the lambs).
- **Railway Services** -- Periodic services held on Garsdale Station platform, sometimes with the Hawes Silver Band. In 2014 a service celebrated the 25th anniversary of the saving of the Settle-Carlisle line, attracting 75 people.
- **Coffee Mornings** -- At Sedbergh URC to benefit the Village Hall.

## History

### The Settle-Carlisle Railway
Mount Zion Chapel was built in 1876 by the very railway contractors (Groves and Woodiwiss) who were building the Settle-Carlisle line. The foundation stone was laid on the day of the first passenger train. Garsdale Station (then called Hawes Junction) was where the Wensleydale branch diverged.

### The Great Flood of 1889
On 8th August 1889, extremely heavy rain fell for over three hours. The hillside on Baugh Fell in Grisedale burst open, releasing millions of gallons of water. Eight stone bridges and seven wooden ones were washed away entirely, two sections of the main road disappeared, and the school was flooded to over four feet depth -- though all children were carried to safety. The Winn family of Ing Heads rebuilt their own bridge, then worked down the dale repairing others, eventually establishing a building partnership in Sedbergh.

### Literary Connections
- **William Wordsworth** and his sister Dorothy walked through Garsdale in 1799 and stayed at Garsdale Hall.
- **"Twixt Moor and Mead"** (1887) by Rev James Dodd Jackson -- stories of Garsdale life based on tales told by his grandparents at Slack Cottage. Places and characters are deliberately identifiable ("Rowansdale" = Garsdale, "Mossgill" = Low House).
- **"The Death of Grass"** (1956) by John Christopher -- a post-apocalyptic novel with a passage describing the journey down Garsdale from Wensleydale, past Mossdale Head to Sedbergh. Made into the film "No Blade of Grass" (1970) and adapted for BBC Radio 4 in 2009.
- **Andrew Lloyd Webber** attended a funeral at Garsdale Church -- William Garnett's father wrote "Aspects of Love," the source material for Lloyd Webber's musical.
- **Prince Charles** occasionally stayed with friends near Garsdale Station.

### Viking Heritage
Houses in Garsdale have Norse names, reflecting Viking settlement heritage.

### Grisedale: "The Dale that Died"
The valley to the north that lost its entire population. Houses there include Blakemire, Mouse Syke, Chapel House, Beck House, Reachey, Aldershaw, Moor Rigg, East House, and Fea Fow.

## Dry Stone Walls

Garsdale has a good selection of dry stone walls, all visible from the public road. Each wall has a distinctive style that identifies the waller who built it.

## Garsdale Dialect

An extensive glossary of traditional dialect words has been preserved, many still in use. A selection:

- **addle** = earn
- **bairn/barn** = child
- **brant** = steep
- **cleg** = horse fly
- **fettle** = working order
- **ginnel** = narrow passage
- **heeaf** = home; the learnt area which sheep graze
- **laik** = play
- **mowdy** = mole
- **neb** = beak, nose, or peak of a cap
- **smoot** = low door for sheep
- **sneck** = door fastener
- **syke** = drain
- **tewit** = lapwing
- **thrang** = busy
