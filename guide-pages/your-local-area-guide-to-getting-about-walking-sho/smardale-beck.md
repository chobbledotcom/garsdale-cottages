    *7 miles/11.3km | 4 hours | moderate*

    This walk follows a disused railway line through a secluded gorge in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, crossing the Smardale Gill Viaduct - built in 1861, rising almost 30 metres on 14 stone arches above Smardale Beck. The viaduct is a fine vantage point for spotting wildlife in the National Nature Reserve, including sparrowhawks, buzzards and treecreepers. The reserve is also home to red squirrels, and the woodland along the beck is one of the better places in the area to see them.

    The route is along the old trackbed, so it is level and well-graded, making it a good option for a steady walk without much climbing. The walk starts from the parking area at Smardale and follows the old line to the viaduct, then continues along the beck before returning. It is about 30 minutes' drive from Garsdale, and the nearest town is Kirkby Stephen, which has pubs, cafes and shops. The [Market Square in Kirkby Lonsdale](/guide/your-local-area-guide-to-getting-about-walking-sho/market-square-kirkby-lonsdale/) is a different Kirkby - closer to Garsdale - but Smardale is worth the drive.

    - **Distance:** 7 miles/11.3km
    - **Parking:** Smardale NNR car park
