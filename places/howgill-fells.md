          *10.5 miles/17km | 5-7 hours | challenging*
          
          The Howgill Fells rise directly above Sedbergh, around fifteen minutes' drive from Garsdale, and they are the closest real mountain walking to both our cottages. The highest point is The Calf at 676 metres, and the standard loop from Sedbergh takes in Winder, Arant Haw and The Calf, covering around 10.5 miles with 2,810 feet of ascent. The Howgills are grassy, rounded and quiet compared to the limestone Dales - you will see few other walkers, and the paths are smooth turf rather than broken stone, which makes for easier going underfoot.
          
          The views from The Calf stretch west over Cumbria and the Lake District, and east over North Yorkshire. The ridge can be confusing in poor visibility because the tops are broad and featureless, so take a map. The route from Sedbergh follows the River Rawthey upstream to Cautley, where you can detour up to [Cautley Spout](/places/cautley-spout/) - England's highest waterfall by total drop - before climbing onto the ridge. The [Cross Keys Temperance Inn](/places/cross-keys-temperance-inn-cautley/) sits at the foot of Cautley Spout if you want a meal and a rest before or after the climb.
          
          Park at the pay-and-display car park in Sedbergh, near the [Tourist Information Centre](/places/sedbergh-tourist-information/). The [Black Bull Inn](/places/black-bull-inn-sedbergh/) and the [Dalesman](/places/dalesman-country-inn-sedbergh/) are both on Main Street for food afterwards.

          ## Shorter Howgill Fells Walks

          Not everyone wants seven hours on the tops, and there are good shorter walks on the Howgill Fells from Sedbergh and from the Cautley side.

          - **Winder from Sedbergh** - about 3.4 miles there and back, two to three hours. Winder is the fell directly behind the town, 473 metres high with a trig point, and locals just call it "the Fell". Follow Howgill Lane out of Sedbergh to the signed permissive path up through Lockbank Farm, and from the top you can see the Lake District, Morecambe Bay on a clear day and back east up the Rawthey towards Garsdale. It is steep for the first half hour and then easy going on grass.
          - **Winder and Arant Haw** - about 7 miles from Sedbergh, carrying on along the ridge from Winder to Arant Haw before dropping back down. This is a good half-day if the full loop to The Calf is too much.
          - **Cautley Spout from the Cross Keys** - park in the lay-by on the A683 near the [Cross Keys Temperance Inn](/places/cross-keys-temperance-inn-cautley/), cross the footbridge over the Rawthey and follow the beck up to the foot of [Cautley Spout](/places/cautley-spout/). It is about a mile each way to the bottom of the falls and mostly flat, and the path up beside the falls to the top is steep. You can carry on to The Calf from there and come back the same way, which is about 5.5 miles in all.

          ## What the Howgills Are Like

          The Howgills are a different sort of hill from the rest of the Yorkshire Dales. They are made of old slates and gritstones rather than limestone, so there are no scars or pavements, and the fells are steep-sided and rounded with very few walls or fences on the tops. Alfred Wainwright, who wrote a book about them, compared them to a herd of sleeping elephants, and the description makes sense once you have seen them from the M6 through the Lune Gorge.

          The southern half of the Howgill Fells has been in the Yorkshire Dales National Park since it was first drawn, and the northern half was brought in when the park was extended in August 2016. All of the Howgills are in Cumbria, not Yorkshire, which confuses people, and Sedbergh and Garsdale were both in the old West Riding of Yorkshire until 1974.

          The fells are open common land, grazed by sheep and by herds of semi-wild Fell ponies that belong to local farmers and roam the tops all year. You will often meet the ponies on the path between Winder and The Calf, and they should be left alone and not fed.

          ## Walking the Howgill Fells with a Dog

          The Howgill Fells are good dog walking country, and all three of our Garsdale holiday cottages are dog friendly, so plenty of our guests take their dogs up Winder or along to The Calf. The grass is easy on paws, and there are becks to drink from on most routes. Dogs need to be on a lead around the sheep and the Fell ponies, which is most of the time up there, and especially in lambing season from around March to May and when ground-nesting birds are on the fells between March and July. The [Red Lion](/places/red-lion-sedbergh/) and the [Thirsty Rambler](/places/thirsty-rambler-sedbergh/) in Sedbergh are both dog friendly for afterwards, and our [dog friendly holiday cottages](/dog-friendly-cottages-in-garsdale-or-yorkshire-dales-stays/) page has more local walks and dog friendly pubs.

          ## Getting to the Howgills from Garsdale

          The Howgill Fells are around fifteen minutes' drive west of our cottages, down the A684 through Garsdale to Sedbergh. For the Cautley side, turn right in Sedbergh onto the A683 towards Kirkby Stephen, and the Cross Keys is four or five miles up the road on the right.
