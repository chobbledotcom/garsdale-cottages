    content: White Scar Cave is near Ingleton in the western Dales, and is the longest show cave in Britain. Guided tours run throughout the day and last about eighty minutes, taking in a series of underground chambers, formations, streams and a notable cavern called the Battlefield, with its flowstone formations. The cave is a more structured experience than the natural local caves around Garsdale such as Tom Croft Cave and Dovecote Cave - electric lighting throughout, no need to bring a torch, and a guide to point out the geology.       White Scar Cave is the longest show cave in Britain, set in the Yorkshire Dales National Park on the B6255 road, about two miles from the village of Ingleton in North Yorkshire. The cave was discovered in 1923 and opened to the public shortly after, and the guided tour takes around eighty minutes through a mile of underground passages, stalactites, stalagmites and flowstone formations. The best-known feature is the Battlefield, a vast natural cavern with thousands of delicate white stalactites, and there is the Judge's Head, a stalagmite that looks uncannily like a bewigged judge in profile.

      The cave stays at a steady 8°C all year round, so a jumper or jacket is a good idea whatever the weather above ground. There is a large car park beside the road and the cave entrance and visitor centre are a two-minute walk from it. The B6255 runs from Ingleton over to Hawes, so the cave pairs well with a day out in Upper Wensleydale if you are staying in Garsdale.
