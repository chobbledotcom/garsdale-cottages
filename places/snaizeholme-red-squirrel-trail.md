          ## How the Red Squirrel Reserve Started

          Snaizeholme is a small side valley off Widdale, a few miles south-west of Hawes at the top of Wensleydale, and the woodland there is part of the Widdale Red Squirrel Reserve. Many of the trees were planted by Hugh and Jane Kemp, who bought the derelict hill farm at Mirk Pot in the 1960s and planted it with Christmas trees. As the conifers grew, red squirrels moved in for the cones, and the farm was later turned into a red squirrel refuge with help from the Forestry Commission and Defra. The Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority worked with the landowners to put in a public viewing area with feeders, because red squirrels are hard to spot in thick spruce woodland unless something draws them out.

          ## Getting to Snaizeholme

          Parking is the awkward part. There is no car park at Snaizeholme, the road is narrow and serves several private properties, and there are only two parking spaces at the red squirrel trail, which have to be booked in advance through the National Park Centre at the [Dales Countryside Museum](/places/dales-countryside-museum-hawes/) in Hawes. Please do not park on the verges or in gateways, because farmers need them.

          The easier way in is the Little White Bus, a community bus from Hawes that will drop you at the top of the track to the trail on its Snaizeholme run. From the drop-off it is a circular walk of about 2.5 miles to the red squirrel viewing area and back, and it takes around forty minutes to walk in from Mirk Pot. The track is rough in places and can be muddy, so wear boots. There are no toilets or cafe at Snaizeholme, so use the facilities in Hawes first. There is a donation box at the viewing area, which goes towards the feeders.

          ## Tips for Seeing Red Squirrels

          Early morning and late afternoon are usually the busiest times at the feeders, and autumn is particularly good because the squirrels are out collecting and burying food for the winter. Once you are at the viewing area, sit still and keep quiet for a while, as they tend to come back to the feeders once people settle down. Binoculars help, and a camera with a decent zoom is worth bringing. Red squirrels moult into thicker coats with ear tufts over the winter, so they look quite different in January from how they look in August.

          ## Visiting Snaizeholme with a Dog

          All three of our Garsdale holiday cottages are dog friendly, and the walk in to Snaizeholme is fine with a dog on a lead. Keep the dog on a short lead and calm at the viewing area itself, because a dog sniffing round the feeders is the quickest way to send every squirrel up the nearest tree. There are sheep in the fields on the way in as well. If your dog is likely to bark or pull, it can be easier for one person to wait with it a little way back while the others watch.

          ## Red Squirrels Closer to Home

          Snaizeholme is the reliable option, but you do not have to drive anywhere to see red squirrels from our cottages. Garsdale is in the core area for reds around Sedbergh, and they are regularly seen in the woods by Dandra Garth, along the River Clough and in the cottage gardens, where they come to the feeders. Our [red squirrels in Garsdale](/red-squirrels-in-garsdale/) page has the best local spots and how to report a sighting. A trip to Snaizeholme also goes well with a visit to the [Wensleydale Creamery](/places/wensleydale-creamery-hawes/) in Hawes, which is only a few miles away.
