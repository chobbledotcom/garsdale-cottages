          The Wensleydale Creamery visitor centre in Hawes is the home of Yorkshire Wensleydale cheese, and the only place in Wensleydale still making the traditional cheese. The factory on this site dates from the early twentieth century, and by 1990 it was the last Wensleydale cheesemaker left in the dale. It is now an award-winning traditional cheese-maker and specialist blender, owned since 2021 by the Canadian dairy group Saputo, and the visitor centre is built around watching the cheese being made.
          
          The Yorkshire Wensleydale Cheese Experience includes a cheesemaking demonstration and a look at the history of the trade, from farmhouse production to the modern creamery. The cheese and gift shop stocks over twenty varieties of award-winning artisan cheese alongside accompaniments, and there is a coffee shop and restaurant serving cheese-inspired refreshments. There is a large dedicated car park on site, and the creamery is a short walk from the centre of Hawes.

          ## A Short History of Wensleydale Cheese
          
          Wensleydale cheese goes back a long way before the creamery. It was first made by Cistercian monks who came over from France and settled at Jervaulx Abbey, further down the dale, in the twelfth century, and they made it from ewes' milk. When the monasteries were dissolved the farmers' wives of Wensleydale carried on making it, by then mostly from cows' milk, and it stayed a farmhouse cheese for the next few hundred years.
          
          The first creamery in Hawes was built in 1897 by Edward Chapman, a local corn merchant, so that milk from the dale's farms could be made into cheese on a bigger scale. It got into trouble in the 1930s depression and was saved in 1935 by Kit Calvert, a Hawes man who called a meeting in the town hall and talked the local farmers into putting money in. The creamery still makes a cheese named after him. He sold it to the Milk Marketing Board in 1966.
          
          In May 1992 Dairy Crest, by then the owner, closed the Hawes creamery with the loss of 59 jobs and moved Wensleydale production over the border into Lancashire, which caused a fair bit of upset in the dale. Six months later four of the managers and a local businessman, John Gibson, bought the creamery back and started making cheese in Hawes again. A few years later Wallace and Gromit gave the cheese a lot of free publicity, with Wallace naming Wensleydale as his favourite in A Close Shave in 1995, and the creamery still sells a Wallace and Gromit range in the shop.
          
          ## Visiting the Creamery with a Dog
          
          All three of our Garsdale holiday cottages are dog friendly, so it is worth knowing that only assistance dogs are allowed inside the Wensleydale Creamery visitor centre itself, which includes the cheese experience, the shop and the restaurant. Dogs are welcome in the outside areas, and the coffee shop garden is dog friendly, with water bowls and views up the dale. If there are two of you, one can sit in the garden with the dog while the other goes round the cheese shop. For dog friendly pubs in Hawes, the [Board Inn](/places/board-inn-hawes/) takes dogs, and our [dog friendly holiday cottages](/dog-friendly-cottages-in-garsdale-or-yorkshire-dales-stays/) page covers the rest of the area.
          
          ## Making a Day of It from Garsdale
          
          The Wensleydale Creamery is about nine miles east of our cottages along the A684, around a twenty-minute drive through Garsdale Head and past the Moorcock Inn. Most people combine it with the rest of Hawes, but there is plenty more within a few miles. The [Snaizeholme Red Squirrel Trail](/places/snaizeholme-red-squirrel-trail/) is just south-west of Hawes and is the most reliable place round here to see red squirrels. [Hardraw Force](/places/hardraw-force/), a mile or so north of Hawes, is reached through the Green Dragon pub. If you carry on south over Newby Head on the B6255, [Ribblehead Viaduct](/places/ribblehead-viaduct/) is around twenty minutes from Hawes, so a cheese stop, the viaduct and the Station Inn make a decent round trip on a day when the tops are clagged in.
